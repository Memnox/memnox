# @memnox/autonomy

The only part of the product that says an agent can safely be given **more** authority
than it has — and the reason that is a level a person granted, never a score.

## Levels, not scores

A scalar that silently widens permission is unauditable and impossible to explain to a
regulator. A level is a named bundle of rules: readable, testable, diffable, revocable.

```
0 observe   1 suggest   2 act reversibly
3 act within bounds   4 act autonomously   5 hold delegated authority
```

**Down is automatic, up is not.** `demote()` needs an incident and nobody's signature.
`promote()` refuses without both a met readiness checklist and a named person — trust
never widens authority on its own, and the moment it did, the product would have removed
the accountable human it sells.

## Readiness is queries

Every item resolves against something already stored, so nobody can tick one, and an
item nothing answers yet is `unknown` rather than a pass. `blockers()` names what is
stopping a level and the change that closes each.

## Two kinds of number, kept apart

`RoleEconomics` reports actions, interventions, retries and spend, all **measured**.
Hours saved and value delivered are **modelled** from a rate the customer supplies, and
`humanRatePerHour` is theirs — presenting it in our own voice would be the same mistake
as an estimated risk exposure.

## Standing limit

Self-proposing is the ceiling in perpetuity. `synthesizeRule()` drafts an ordinary
proposal from repeated identical approvals and one dissent resets it. There is no version
of this product that enacts its own rules.
